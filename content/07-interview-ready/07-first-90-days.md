# Your first 90 days as an FDE
<!-- order: 2.9 -->
<!-- minutes: 30 -->

Some loops ask: *"You join us and are sent to a new customer next week. What do you do in your first 90 days?"* It is also the plan you will really need. A strong answer shows you **listen first, deliver something small early, and build trust before you build big**.

## Days 1–30: learn and earn trust

- Learn the product deeply: do the internal training, deploy it yourself, read the last 3 customer handover notes.
- Meet the customer's people: sponsor, users, IT, security, data owners. Ask each: "What does success look like for you? What worries you?"
- Map the systems, the data and the approval processes (security review, change board, data access).
- **Ship one small, visible win** in the first 2–3 weeks: fix a painful bug, automate a report, unblock a data feed.
- Write down: goals, stakeholders, risks, and a draft plan. Share it and ask "What did I get wrong?"

## Days 31–60: deliver the first milestone

- Agree on the first milestone with a success metric (lesson 6.1).
- Weekly status updates (lesson 6.5) with honest RAG status.
- Build with the users: show work every week, not at the end.
- Feed product gaps back to your own engineering team with clear, prioritised write-ups.

## Days 61–90: scale and hand over

- Measure the milestone against the metric and present the result to the sponsor.
- Plan the next phase together; turn your notes into a handover document (lesson 5c.3).
- Make yourself less necessary: train the customer's team, document runbooks, set up monitoring and alerts.

:::written Your 90-day plan
Write your 90-day plan for this situation (about 300 words): you join an AI company as an FDE and are placed with a large UK university that wants an assistant to answer researchers' questions about computing and data services, with strict data governance.
--- rubric ---
- Days 1–30 focus on learning (product, people, systems, governance) and include a small early win
- Names the right stakeholders (research computing, information security, data protection officer, researchers, IT service desk) and what to ask them
- A first milestone with a measurable metric (for example share of questions answered correctly with citations, tickets deflected) and a pilot group
- Governance from the start (DPIA, data sources, access control, logging)
- Regular communication and feedback to the product team
- Days 61–90 measure, plan the next phase and hand over (documentation, training, monitoring)
:::
