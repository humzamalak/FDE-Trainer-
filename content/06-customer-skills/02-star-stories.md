# STAR stories and your career story
<!-- minutes: 60 -->

Behavioural questions test **ownership, judgement and how you work with people**. Answer with **STAR**:

- **S**ituation (1–2 sentences): the context.
- **T**ask (1 sentence): what *you* had to do.
- **A**ction (most of the answer): what **you** did — say "I", not "we". Include the technical detail and the decisions.
- **R**esult (1–2 sentences): numbers if possible, and what you learned.

Keep each story to about 2 minutes. Expect 2 follow-up questions on every story.

## Difficult CV questions (gaps, short roles)

Interviewers may ask about a gap or a short role on your CV. **Be short, true and forward-looking.** Do not over-explain or apologise. Practise until you can say it calmly in under 45 seconds.

Structure: **fact → what you learned → evidence of the change.**

> "Early in my career I had a short role that did not work out. I learned to agree expectations in the first weeks and to ask for feedback early. Since then I have done that, for example [evidence]. Last year I took a planned career break, then did independent projects before my current role."

Adapt this to what is true for you, and ask the tutor to stress-test it. Your written answers stay on your computer (in `data/`, which git ignores).

## Questions to ask them at the end

- "What does a great first 90 days look like in this role?"
- "How do FDEs and the product team work together on customer feedback?"
- "What is the hardest deployment the team did recently?"

:::written Write your first STAR story
Write a STAR story for "Tell me about a time you owned a project from start to finish." Use the Open OnDemand / H200 cluster work or another real project. 200–300 words. Use "I". Include at least one number.
--- rubric ---
- Clear Situation and Task in a few sentences
- Action is the largest part, in first person, with specific technical decisions and why
- Shows ownership beyond the minimum (e.g. infrastructure as code, documentation, stakeholder communication)
- Result with at least one concrete number or measurable outcome, plus a learning
- Length within 200–300 words and easy to say aloud in about 2 minutes
:::

:::written Practise the hard question
Write your honest answer to: "I see a gap and some short roles on your CV. Can you tell me about those?" Keep it under 100 words.
--- rubric ---
- Short, factual and calm; no over-explaining or blaming others
- Shows learning from the short roles and gives evidence of change since
- Explains the gap briefly and positively (career break, then independent projects)
- Ends forward-looking, linking back to current strengths or the role
- Under 100 words
:::
